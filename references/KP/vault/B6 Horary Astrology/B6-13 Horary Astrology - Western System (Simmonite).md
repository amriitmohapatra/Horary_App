# Horary Astrology - Western System (Simmonite)

[[J_KP reader_6_Horary Astrology.pdf#page=71|p. 71]]

Many authors have published many books on Horary Astrology. It has been widely practised by great scientists and very learned scholars. The western system is explained most scientifically and their arguments convince the readers.

It has already been said in this book that in the western system, the commencement of the zodiac, differs from that of the Hindu system; thereby the signs get changed. Some of the planets are considered to occupy different signs; so, the lords of the signs and the qualities as well as characteristics attributed to the signs get changed. The westerners erect the horoscopes according to Sayana system whereas we erect according to Nirayana. They do not include the Nodes, while offering prediction, whereas they take Uranus, Neptune and Pluto for judgment.

The following extract is taken from the book "Horary Astrology" by Dr. W. J. SIMONITE M.D., Ph.D., with additions by John Story and edited by ERNEST A. GRANT.

**1. "The time of erecting a horary figure"**

a. Many disputes have arisen about the proper time for the figure of a horary question to be erected. Some think the moment when the querent is first seen by the artist; others when he salutes him, others when he inquires of him, etc. But the proper time is the hour and the minute when the querent feels the most anxious about the matter.

b. If a person apply to the Astrologer the figure must be erected for the minute the querent proposes the question.

[[J_KP reader_6_Horary Astrology.pdf#page=72|p. 72]]

c. If a letter be sent to an artist the time of the letter being received is not taken notice of, but the moment the artist reads the very questions.

d. In sickness the time must be taken when the person first spoke to the physician concerning the disease, whether the querent is the afflicted party or not.

e. If a parent bring the water of a child, though the child cannot speak, the 1st house represents the child, and not the 5th, and so of the rest.

f. If it be not a question but a sudden event, take the moment of its commencement, as the setting off on a journey, beginning a letter, or any business, etc., or when you first discover the loss of an article. In all these cases the first impression on your mind is the time and moment for the figure.

**2.** The following extract is taken from the book "Modern Text-book of Astrology" by MARGARET E. HONE (page 272).

If the question comes to an astrologer's mind, he casts the chart for the moment of his thought, using the latitude and longitude of the place where he is. If a person comes to an astrologer and puts his request, the time of the request is taken.

Diversity of opinion exists as to the procedure to follow when there is delay through the post. Some astrologers say that the time of opening the letter should be taken, others stating that the querent should give the time when the idea came to birth in his own mind. This seems the more reasonable way.

The chart having been cast, the ruler of the sign on the first cusp is reckoned to represent the querent, and the ruler of the sign on the cusp of the house connected with the affairs of the question is taken as its significator. "This is the age of enquiry and yet prejudice continues to press down her leaden foot upon the neck of examination in the matter. ## What Each Planet Signifies

**Neptune** can produce ideal people with religious bent of mind and psychic powers. It can also produce lethargic, lazy, irresponsible, and immoral people who will cheat others and be dishonest.

It indicates poets, anaesthetists, chemists, detectives, liquids, oils, and those who work in oil field, gas, fish, sea, hospitals, jails, caves, submarine, etc.

**Uranus** indicates intelligent or eccentric people, fortune, violence, sudden changes, invention, novelty, harmony or divorce, astrologers, teachers, lecturers, sculptors, those who hypnotise, mesmerise, those who work in railways, banks, gas plants, asylum, dispensaries, research laboratories, aerodrome etc.

**Saturn:** imaginative, cautious action, silence, reserved nature, patience, preserving, studious, sincere, faithful; or envious, jealousy, untruthful, lazy, liar, discontentment, dishonest; also those who work in woods, unfrequented places, old churches, dilapidated buildings, cess pools, drains, sewerage.

**Jupiter** produces magnanimous, noble, honourable, true, reliable people, with ambition and hopeful disposition. Their dealings will ever be fair and their mind will be to help others. They are religious and god-fearing. They try to stick on to tradition. They lead a very happy family life with their children; they are charitable and sympathetic. Jupiter indicates judges, advocates, professors, priests, ministers, administrators, chancellors, civilians, bankers, revenue depts, etc; also those who work in palace, gardens, law courts, etc., those who deal in honey, oil, silk, clothing, horse, domestic fowls etc.

**Mars:** Brave, violent, unyielding, taking chances, pleasure in fighting, over liberal, extravagant. It shows captains, soldiers, surgeons, chemists, barbers, butchers, thieves, smiths, tailors, carpenters, masons, etc. It also denotes those who work in fire, furnaces, ovens, or deal in blood, and so on.

[[J_KP reader_6_Horary Astrology.pdf#page=75|p. 75]]

**Sun:** produces liberal, noble, and sympathetic people, reliable, proud and generous. They are fond of dress, jewels, internal decorations, and also outside; those who work in the Government in any capacity, magistrates, stewards, goldsmiths, copper-smiths, mint, municipalities, corporation, etc.

**Venus:** Calm, level headed, ever-smiling, pleasing, never irritating, sweet, cheerful, dance, music, silk, linen, paint, jewel, embroidery, women, perfumery, engraving, bed, dance, banqueting hall.

**Mercury:** Intelligent, logician, studious, industrious, research minded, witty, learning without an instructor, travellers, good knowledge in occult sciences, astrologers, mathematicians, travelling agents, poets, advocates, dress, printers, publishers, orators, ambassadors, clerks, messengers, schools, assemblies, libraries.

**Moon:** Ever changing habits, unsteady, thinks of the present alone, timid, tries to lead a peaceful life, tries to avoid anxiety, worry, etc. travellers, fish, breweries, milk, messengers, marine, navy, those who deal in liquids. (They do not take Rahu or Kethu.)
